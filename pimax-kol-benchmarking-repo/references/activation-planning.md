# Partner activation planning

Use this reference after comparison analysis when the output will be handed to a KOL/BD owner. The goal is a runnable collaboration, not a list of content ideas.

## Translate diagnosis into an action

For every proposed action, include five fields:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Partner evidence | The partner strength or gap observed in their own content/data |
| Benchmark evidence | The behavior or mechanism learned from the target performer, with a link when available |
| Action | The exact deliverable, responsibility, and timing |
| Why this action | How the comparison logically supports the action; do not use generic best practice as the only reason |
| Intended effect | The specific exposure, viewing-quality, search, click, order, product-mix, or measurement change expected |

Do not claim that the intended effect will occur. Treat it as a testable expectation and specify how it will be observed.

## Start with a capability conversation

Before finalizing a brief, give the business owner a short meeting or message script that:

- references one or two real partner examples;
- explains why their audience or format fits the proposed mechanism;
- proposes a content problem, not a fixed script;
- confirms product access, equipment, format, schedule, and creative capacity;
- confirms tracking, disclosure, and backend-data sharing before shipment or signature;
- explains what Pimax will provide and what feedback the partner will receive.

Do not write deceptive personalization or pretend to have watched evidence that was not inspected.

## Specify one primary plan and one conditional follow-up

The primary plan should contain:

- business and audience objective;
- exact content role and deliverables;
- title/topic directions while preserving creator control;
- opening/hook expectation;
- mandatory proof and test conditions;
- CTA, link/code, landing path, and disclosure;
- Pimax inputs and named owner;
- pre-production, publication, 7-day, and 30-day checkpoints for YouTube; use 30/90 days for Blog where search maturation requires it;
- data fields requested at each checkpoint;
- scale, iterate, reposition, or stop decision.

The follow-up plan should be conditional: state which first-plan signal activates it. Examples include a settings tutorial after a successful hero test, a comparison after strong high-intent comments, or a cluster page after a review begins receiving non-brand impressions.

## Use partner-relative baselines

Before publication, establish a baseline from comparable content rather than all channel output:

- YouTube: recent comparable non-live videos, matched-age views, impressions, CTR, first-30-second retention, average view duration, traffic sources, clicks, and orders where available.
- Blog: comparable page/query impressions, clicks, CTR, position, product clicks, orders, and update age.

Define experimental thresholds as internal decision rules, not industry benchmarks. If the baseline is missing, make data collection the first gate instead of inventing a target.

## Diagnose the result before requesting more content

Use the funnel location to choose the next action:

| Observed result | Likely constraint | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| High impressions, low CTR | Packaging or query/topic mismatch | Revise title/thumbnail/snippet and re-evaluate |
| Adequate CTR, weak early retention | Opening, promise delivery, or early sponsorship | Recut/restructure the opening before repeating the topic |
| Strong viewing/search, weak clicks | CTA, product fit, landing path, price, or offer | Fix the conversion path; do not simply demand another post |
| Strong clicks, weak orders | Landing page, price, availability, trust, or product-market fit | Audit onsite and commercial factors |
| Strong exposure and qualified sales | Transferable mechanism with commercial support | Execute the conditional follow-up and test with another matched partner |
| Missing backend/tracking | Measurement failure | Instrument first; keep commercial conclusions unknown |

## Communication and follow-up pack

Return:

1. initial outreach or meeting opener;
2. confirmation questions;
3. one-page collaboration brief;
4. responsibility and timeline table;
5. publication-day checklist;
6. exact data-request message for each checkpoint;
7. result interpretation and next-decision rules.

The communication should be ready to send after the business owner fills only genuine unknowns such as dates, compensation, product version, hardware, and contact name.
